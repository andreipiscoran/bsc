# Experimental Results

**Deepfake and Synthetic-Image Detection via Handcrafted Features**
B.Sc. Thesis — Babeș-Bolyai University
**Author:** Andrei Piscoran

This document reports the principal empirical findings of the thesis. All
figures are drawn from the analysis and benchmark artifacts generated by the
`feature_benchmark` pipeline and are reproducible from the scripts described in
the repository `README`.

---

## 1. Overview

The study evaluates whether seven families of handcrafted image features —
Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT), intensity, colour, texture (LBP/GLCM/Gabor),
wavelet, noise-residue, and Fourier (FFT) descriptors — can separate authentic
images from AI-generated or manipulated ones. Two datasets are used: **CIFAKE**
(natural photographs vs. generative-model images) and **FaceForensics++**
(authentic vs. manipulated face video frames).

Three experimental axes are reported:

1. **Statistical separability** of each feature family on CIFAKE (distributional
   distance and significance testing).
2. **Discriminative classification** performance of linear models on CIFAKE at
   full scale (120,000 samples).
3. **Generalisation**, comprising a single-dataset Linear Discriminant Analysis
   (LDA) study on FaceForensics++ and a cross-dataset transfer experiment.

---

## 2. Statistical Separability on CIFAKE

Feature distributions for the real and fake classes were compared using the
Kolmogorov–Smirnov (KS) test, Hotelling's *T²* statistic, covariance-difference
norms, and KL divergence. The table below summarises a 5,000-sample analysis,
ranked by the number of individual feature dimensions reaching significance at
α = 0.05.

| Feature family | Significant dims (α = 0.05) | Hotelling *T²* | Mean KS | KL divergence |
|----------------|:---------------------------:|:--------------:|:-------:|:-------------:|
| **Colour**     | **24** | 6,438.0 | 0.136 | 5.26 |
| Texture        | 15     | 9,027.2 | 0.137 | 1.81 |
| Intensity      | 13     | 2,442.2 | 0.108 | 1.26 |
| Wavelet        | 12     | 2,788.1 | 0.122 | 1.41 |
| DCT            | 10     | 4,518.0 | 0.169 | 4.99 |

**Findings.** At realistic sample sizes every feature family carries
statistically significant signal. Colour descriptors separate the two classes
across the largest number of dimensions, while texture features yield the
highest multivariate Hotelling *T²*, indicating the strongest aggregate mean
separation. A control run on only 20 samples produced almost no significant
dimensions, confirming that the separability is a genuine population effect
rather than a small-sample artifact and underscoring the need for adequate
sample sizes in forensic feature studies.

## 3. Linear Classification on CIFAKE

A logistic-regression classifier (`StandardScaler` → `LogisticRegression`,
`liblinear`) was trained on the full CIFAKE set of 120,000 samples with an
84,000 / 36,000 train/test split. Two feature configurations were compared: a
noise-only baseline and a tuned noise + texture fusion.

| Configuration | Dims | Accuracy | F1 | ROC-AUC | Precision | Recall |
|---------------|:----:|:--------:|:--:|:-------:|:---------:|:------:|
| Noise (baseline) | 48 | 0.8554 | 0.8539 | 0.9292 | 0.8631 | 0.8449 |
| **Noise + Texture (tuned, C = 8)** | 63 | **0.8714** | **0.8702** | **0.9432** | **0.8780** | **0.8626** |

**Findings.** Noise-residue features alone achieve an 85.5 % accuracy and a
0.929 ROC-AUC, confirming that generative artifacts leave a detectable
high-frequency signature. Fusing texture descriptors and tuning the
regularisation strength via 5-fold cross-validation raises accuracy by **+1.6
percentage points** and ROC-AUC to **0.943**. Crucially, the held-out test
ROC-AUC (0.9432) matches the cross-validated training estimate (0.9429),
indicating no overfitting or leakage. Training the linear model takes under four
seconds and inference under 20 ms over 36,000 samples, demonstrating that the
approach is computationally inexpensive relative to deep-learning detectors.

## 4. Single-Dataset Results on FaceForensics++

Per-family LDA accuracy was measured on 2,000 FaceForensics++ frames.

| Feature family | LDA accuracy |
|----------------|:------------:|
| **Colour**     | **0.6217** |
| FFT            | 0.6083 |
| Noise          | 0.6067 |
| Wavelet        | 0.5850 |
| Intensity      | 0.5642 |
| DCT            | 0.5583 |
| Texture        | 0.5525 |

**Findings.** Detection on FaceForensics++ is substantially harder than on
CIFAKE. The best single family (colour) reaches only 62 % accuracy. This is
consistent with the nature of the data: FaceForensics++ manipulations are
localised face swaps and reenactments embedded in otherwise authentic video
frames, so global statistical cues are weaker than in fully synthesised CIFAKE
images. An earlier 100-sample pilot reported accuracies near 0.90; the drop at
2,000 samples illustrates the optimism bias of small evaluation sets and the
importance of reporting results at scale.

## 5. Cross-Dataset Generalisation

To test transfer, the noise-feature linear model trained on CIFAKE (120,000
samples, 48 dimensions) was evaluated directly on FaceForensics++ (400 samples)
with no adaptation.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|:-----:|
| Accuracy | 0.500 |
| Balanced accuracy | 0.500 |
| ROC-AUC | 0.524 |
| Matthews correlation (MCC) | 0.000 |
| Predicted-fake rate | 0.900 |

Confusion matrix `[[TN, FP], [FN, TP]]` = `[[20, 180], [20, 180]]`.

**Findings.** Cross-dataset transfer effectively collapses to chance: balanced
accuracy of 0.50, ROC-AUC of 0.52, and an MCC of 0.00. The model labels 90 % of
the FaceForensics++ frames "fake" regardless of true class, a clear sign of
distribution shift in the noise-feature space between synthetically generated
CIFAKE images and compression-laden manipulated video frames. A label-flip
sanity check produced symmetric results, ruling out a simple class-mapping
error. This is the study's most consequential negative result.

---

## 6. Summary of Key Findings

1. **Handcrafted features are strongly discriminative within a dataset.** A
   lightweight linear model on noise + texture features attains 87.1 % accuracy
   and 0.943 ROC-AUC on CIFAKE, at negligible computational cost.
2. **Noise-residue and texture features are the most useful single families for
   fully synthetic images**, while colour and frequency (FFT) features carry the
   most signal for manipulated face video.
3. **Statistical significance scales with sample size.** Distributional
   separability is robust at realistic sample sizes and largely absent in tiny
   samples — a methodological caution for the field.
4. **Generalisation across datasets is the central limitation.** A detector
   tuned on CIFAKE does not transfer to FaceForensics++ (≈ chance performance),
   demonstrating that handcrafted-feature detectors learn dataset-specific
   generative signatures rather than a universal notion of "fakeness".

These results position handcrafted-feature detection as an efficient,
interpretable, and competitive in-domain method, while motivating
domain-adaptation or hybrid deep-learning approaches to close the
cross-dataset generalisation gap.

| Result | Producing script | Configuration |

ensure repeatability.
